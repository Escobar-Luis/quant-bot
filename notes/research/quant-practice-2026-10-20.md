## 1. Sourced findings
1. Backtesting is not a research tool; if it fails, fix the research process, do not tweak until it passes. López de Prado (@lopezdeprado), 2019-06-12, 316 likes. https://x.com/i/status/1138716396248915968
2. Testing 45+ configs on a 5-year sample almost guarantees an in-sample Sharpe near 1.0 with an expected out-of-sample Sharpe of zero (a theoretical result). Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu, 2014/2015 (PDF read). CXO summary: https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/insidiousness-of-overfitting-investment-strategies-via-iterative-backtests
3. Hold-out is unreliable for investment backtests: you have usually seen the OOS period, it is too short, and one lucky split can mislead. Same paper, "Comparisons to other approaches". https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf
4. Not reporting the number of trials makes overfitting unmeasurable. Hiding failed variants biases every test. Same paper, section on file-drawer problem.
5. Their PBO/CSCV test can't catch wrong costs or data not available at decision time. It assumes the backtest itself is right. Same paper, limitations.
6. A flat 50% Sharpe haircut is crude. Harvey and Liu: the multiple-testing haircut is nonlinear and hits marginal strategies hardest; they also give a minimum profit hurdle. "Backtesting", J. Portfolio Mgmt 2015. https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF
7. Single in-sample/out-of-sample split is "broken by default": re-running 30-47 times contaminates the test set; walk-forward is the workhorse. RohOnChain, 2026-09-11, 252 likes / 17.9k views (influencer, retail author). https://x.com/i/status/2098486986583757043
8. Parameter sensitivity, walk-forward, stress tests and Monte Carlo as the four checks. GoshawkTrades, 2025-11-28, 277 likes / 21k views (retail algo trader, thin credibility). https://x.com/i/status/1994489268937658479
9. "Does it beat random?" Compare your strategy to what luck produces over the same search (Woodriff, Hedge Fund Market Wizards). buildalpha, 2026-10-12, 37 likes (algo-strategy tool vendor). https://x.com/buildalpha/status/2109662458495255034
10. A filter or regime rule that "fixes" backtest drawdowns is uncompensated risk the market never pays you for. 0xaporia, 2026-05-04, 325 likes / 50.9k views. https://x.com/i/status/2051260298506498314
11. Regime filter on a simple SP500 mean-reversion rule lifted average trade and cut time in market; shown as a candidate, not proof. buildalpha, 2026-10-17, 238 likes / 18.7k views. https://x.com/i/status/2111415071863673198
12. Costs alone erase edges: Sharpe 2.30 to 1.49 at 5 bps/side, 0.46 at 20 bps/side; 240 ideas, 0 approved for real money. Second-hand summary of a claimed Stanford paper, so unverified. 0xkvro, 2026-10-12, 476 likes / 38k views. https://x.com/i/status/2109677714756014331
13. Same pattern in a claimed MIT paper: 27 variants, 6 look good in-sample, 2 survive OOS, 0 approved. Unverified. 0xkvro, 2026-10-16, 352 likes. https://x.com/i/status/2111175213832413549
14. Vibe-coded bot losing 0.75 bp per trade is "a machine for transferring your money to people smarter than you". macrocephalopod (quant researcher), 2026-07-26, 2,350 likes / 254k views. https://x.com/i/status/2081476546007535665
15. Small-cap/short friction: retail default slippage understates real friction 10-100x; $1-10 names 25-100 bps; borrow 0.25-2% easy, 5-50% hard, far higher for squeeze names, charged daily, availability flips intraday. Community skill file, rough figures, not authoritative. https://skills.cat/skills/jefrnc/quant-llm-skills/transaction-cost-modeling ; fee mechanics: https://help.en-us.firstrade.com/article/133-what-are-hard-to-borrow-stocks-and-fees-and-when-are-those-fees-charged

## 2. What this means for Day Trader AI (ranked by impact)
1. **Count trials and haircut the result.** Log every variant ever tried per course (the 10 filter variants, 6 model proposals, setups, universe picks) in one file; report trial count beside each verdict and compute Deflated Sharpe / Harvey-Liu hurdle. Applies: `notes/research/backtesting.md` log, `results/*.json` (add `n_trials`, `deflated_sharpe`), verdict code. Rationale: findings 2, 4, 6, 16.
2. **Treat our "held-out" months as spent.** They fall before the model's training cutoff and smoke tests saw them (finding 3). Only data after today, or after the pre-registration commit, counts as clean. Applies: sealed-holdout rule 4; label old holdout "dev-2" and drop the word "sealed" for it.
3. **Forward paper-test from today with a frozen rule file.** Commit rules, universe, costs and threshold before the first trade; no edits; score on a fixed date. Applies: new `app/forward/`, results stamped with commit sha. Rationale: findings 1, 3, 13; this is the only real OOS we can get.
4. **Pre-register the newer-months/other-stocks test and stop after one look.** Commit the universe, entry rule, cost model and pass bar (e.g. beats SPY after costs, 30+ trades, positive in 2 of 3 sub-periods) before running. Any re-run is a new logged trial. Applies: the current "mechanical rules on newer months" test.
5. **Replace fixed 5 bps with a small-cap cost model.** Price-tiered spread plus slippage (25-100 bps on $1-10 names; scale by dollar volume and size vs minute volume), and run a doubled-cost stress. Verdict must survive 2x costs. Applies: contracts item 2 (costs), `app/backtest/`, audit calculator. Rationale: findings 12, 15.
6. **Model shorting honestly: borrow fee, locate, squeeze.** Short entries on hot small caps are the exact case where locates fail and fees reach 5-50%+ a year (daily charge, day-of fee spikes). Add per-name fee assumption, skip-if-not-ETB-proxy flag, and a "trade not fillable" bucket; show P&L with and without. Add a worst-case squeeze check on the stop (gap through stop). Applies: setup tests for run-up shorts and crypto-linked shorts. Rationale: finding 15; also flag as an unknown in `unknown`.
7. **Walk-forward instead of one dev/sealed split.** Rolling train-then-test windows over the 13 months (e.g. 3 train / 1 test, 10 folds); report fold-by-fold consistency, not the pooled number. Applies: filter and setup selection in `app/backtest/`. Rationale: findings 3, 7.
8. **Add a minimum trade count and a significance floor per claim.** A setup or filter needs 30+ trades (our "Not enough trades" line) and a bootstrap or PSR/MinTRL check; with 342 trades total, most sub-slices won't pass. Applies: verdict word logic, learning view cards (hide cards below the floor). Rationale: finding 16.
9. **Add a "vs random" check on every filter and setup.** Shuffle entry days/labels or pick random subsets of the same size 1,000 times; report the percentile of the real result. Cheap and catches post-hoc selection. Applies: audit module, evaluator for PRISM proof 3. Rationale: finding 9.
10. **Regime split before claiming a setup works.** The 13 months were a bull market; short-heavy results may be a window artifact, and a regime filter added after seeing losses is "uncompensated risk" (finding 10). Report results by SPY up/down months and ask whether filters were pre-registered. Applies: results `portfolios`, learning view. Rationale: findings 10, 11.
11. **Parameter-sensitivity band instead of point values.** For each filter threshold, show results at +/-20-50% of the value; reject filters whose edge disappears off the exact number. Applies: the 6 model-proposed filters. Rationale: finding 8.
12. **Position sizing and drawdown: report, don't optimize.** Fixed $1k/trade is fine for the verdict; add max drawdown, longest losing streak and loss-per-trade tail (short squeeze) next to return, and do not tune size on results. Applies: results metrics, verdict detail view. Rationale: findings 14, 6 (profit hurdle).
